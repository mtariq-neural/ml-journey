# Types of Statistics

Statistics is split into two main branches: **Descriptive** and **Inferential**. One summarizes what you already have, while the other makes educated guesses about the bigger picture.

---

## 1. Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics is exactly what it sounds like—it describes the data you already have. Think of it as a snapshot, a leaderboard, or a summary report. It doesn’t try to guess anything outside of the numbers right in front of you. Its only goal is to organize and clean up a messy pile of data so it actually makes sense.

* **Key tools:** Averages, percentages, charts, and graphs.

### Example: The Classroom
If I calculate the average score of my math class on a recent test and find out it is 85%, I am using descriptive statistics. I am simply describing exactly how *this* specific class performed without guessing how anyone else would do.

### Example: Fitness Tracker
When your smartwatch tells you that you took 8,500 steps yesterday, that is descriptive statistics. It is simply organizing and summarizing your actual movement for that single day.

---

## 2. Inferential Statistics

Inferential statistics is where we start making predictions. In the real world, we rarely have the time, money, or ability to measure everyone or everything. Instead, we take a small, manageable slice of data (a **sample**) and use it to make an educated guess about a much larger group (the **population**).

* **Key tools:** Probability, hypothesis testing, and margins of error.

### Example: The Classroom (Part 2)
If I take that 85% average from my specific math class and use it to predict how **all students across the entire city** would do on that same test, I am making an inference. I am using a small group to guess the behavior of a massive group.

### Example: Soup Tasting
When you're cooking a giant pot of soup, you don't drink the whole pot to know if it needs salt. You taste **one spoonful (the sample)** and infer how the **whole pot (the population)** tastes. That is inferential statistics in action.
